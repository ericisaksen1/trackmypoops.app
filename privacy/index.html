Privacy Policy

Last updated October 6, 2026

The short version

  • Your poop log lives on your phone. There's no account and no sign-up.
  • Poop Pals is optional. If you join, the poops you choose to share and the name you pick are stored online so your pals can see them. Leave and it's deleted.
  • The app shows ads from Google AdMob. Google's ad software collects some device and usage data to show and measure ads. It never gets your poop log. Flush Away the Ads turns ads off.
  • The app asks what year you were born, once. The answer stays on your phone. Kids get kid-safe ads and no Poop Pals, and nobody under 18 gets personalized ads.
  • You can delete everything whenever you want.

Who I am

Track My Poops is made by Eric Isaksen ("I" and "me" below). This policy covers the Track My Poops app and this website. Questions? Email trackmypoops@gmail.com.

What stays on your phone

Everything you log (date and time, color, kind, size, stink score and notes) and your settings are saved in the app's storage on your phone. Unless you join Poop Pals, none of it is sent to me or anyone else.

Your birth year. The first time you open the app, it asks what year you were born. The answer is saved on your phone and is never sent to me, Google or anyone else. The app uses it to decide how ads work and whether Poop Pals is available (see Children and teens).

Like other app data, your log can be included in your phone's own backups, like iCloud Backup on iPhone or Google's backup on Android. Those are handled by Apple or Google under your account, not by me.

Export. If you tap Export, the app turns your log into a spreadsheet file (CSV) and opens your phone's share menu. Where it goes from there is up to you.

GIFs and report cards. The flush GIF, the Poop Report and the Poop Wrapped cards are made on your phone and only go where you send them. On iPhone, saving one to your photos asks for permission to add photos first. The app can't see the photos you already have.

Reminders and recaps

Reminders, the weekly Poop Report notice (Sundays at 6:00 PM) and the Poop Wrapped notice (December 1) are scheduled on your phone. Nothing goes to a server for them. Turn them off in the app or in your phone's Settings.

Poop Pals

Poop Pals lets friends see each other's poops. It's off until you join, and it's only for people 13 and older.

  • No sign-up. Joining creates an anonymous account. No email, no password, no phone number. The account belongs to the app on your phone.
  • What's stored: the name you pick, an account ID, your sharing choices, the invite codes you make, and for the last 120 days, which days you went. Plus only what you tick: the time, color, kind and size; the stink score; your notes (off unless you turn it on). Unticked details never leave your phone, and unticking something later removes it from what you already shared. Poops older than 120 days are removed as the app syncs.
  • Who sees it: only your pals. People become pals only when one shares a one-time invite code and the other enters it. Pals see your name, what you share and the reactions on your poops. Database rules stop anyone else from reading it. You can remove a pal anytime.
  • Reactions you send are stored and shown to that pal.
  • Reports: if you report a pal, the report is stored: your account ID, theirs, their name, the reason you picked and the note you reported, if any. Only I can see reports. I use them to deal with people breaking the Poop Pals rules and keep them only as long as that takes.
  • Push alerts: if you allow notifications, your phone's push token is stored so the app can tell you when a pal flushes, reacts or uses your code. Alerts are sent through Expo's push service and Apple's (or Google's). You can turn them off on the Pals screen.
  • Where it lives: a database run for me by Supabase.
  • Leaving: Leave Poop Pals deletes your account and everything you shared: your pals, your shared poops and your reactions. The log on your phone stays. If you delete the app without leaving first, email me your Poop Pals name and a pal's name, and I'll delete your account.

Flush Away the Ads

Flush Away the Ads is a one-time purchase through the App Store or Google Play. Apple or Google handles the payment, and I never see your payment details. The app asks the store what you've bought, so it can turn ads off and bring the purchase back on a new phone.

Ads

Ads keep Track My Poops free. They come from Google AdMob, and Google's ad software (the Google Mobile Ads SDK) runs inside the app. According to Google, it can collect:

  • Your IP address, which can be used to estimate your general location
  • Device identifiers: the advertising identifier (on iPhone, only if you allow tracking; on Android, unless you've deleted it) or an identifier that only works within this app
  • Ad data, like which ads you saw or tapped
  • How you use the app, like launches and taps
  • Diagnostics, like crash logs and performance data

Google uses this to show ads, measure them, cap how often you see the same ad and prevent fraud, and if you allow it, to personalize ads. The ad software never gets your poop log or anything from Poop Pals, and the app never puts anything about your poops into ad requests. If you buy Flush Away the Ads, the app stops requesting ads and skips the consent form (and, on iPhone, the tracking prompt).

Google's use of this data is covered by Google's Privacy Policy and how Google uses information from apps that use its services.

Your ad choices

  • Tracking on iPhone. If you're 18 or older, the app asks whether to allow tracking. If you say no, your advertising identifier isn't shared and ads are less personalized. Change it anytime in Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking.
  • Advertising ID on Android. Reset or delete it in Settings > Privacy > Ads (on some phones, Settings > Google > Ads). Without it, ads are less personalized.
  • Consent. Where the law requires it (for example in the EU, the UK, Switzerland and some US states), Google's consent form appears in the app. You can change your answer later on the app's Reminders screen, under Ads > Ad privacy choices.
  • Google's ad settings: myadcenter.google.com
  • The app asks Google to keep ads at a PG rating or milder (G for kids).

What I get

  • AdMob reports with totals, like how many ads were shown and what they earned.
  • Apple's and Google's sales reports, plus anonymous crash reports and app usage stats from people who agreed to share them with app developers (Share With App Developers on iPhone, usage and diagnostics on Android).
  • Poop Pals data sits in the database I run. I don't browse it, sell it or use it for ads. I'd only look at it to fix a problem or if the law required it.

I don't get your poop log (unless you share it with pals), your location or your payment details.

Selling data

I don't sell your personal information. Some US state laws count ads that are personalized using data from other apps and sites as "selling" or "sharing." Where those laws apply, Google's consent form in the app lets you opt out.

Children and teens

Track My Poops isn't made for children, but kids might still find it, so the first time it opens, it asks everyone what year they were born. A year can't tell whether this year's birthday has happened yet, so the app goes by the younger age it could mean. Born in 2013 and it's 2026? The app treats you as 12.

  • Under 13: Poop Pals is turned off, and if you had already joined, your Poop Pals account and everything you shared are deleted. Every ad request is marked as being for a child, so Google turns off personalized ads and remarketing and shows only ads rated G. The app never asks for tracking permission.
  • 13 to 17: ads are never personalized and the app never asks for tracking permission. Poop Pals works the same as for everyone else.
  • 18 and older: everything works as described above.

The app asks only once. As the years go by, it moves you up on its own. I don't knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you think a child has shared something through Poop Pals, email me and I'll delete it.

Deleting your data

  • Delete your whole log: on the app's Reminders screen, go to Your Data > Delete all.
  • Delete your Poop Pals account and everything you shared: Leave Poop Pals on the Pals screen.
  • Deleting the app removes everything it saved on your phone.
  • Data Google collected for ads is Google's to manage. Use Google's ad settings above.
  • Anything else, email me.

This website

This site doesn't use cookies, analytics or trackers, and its fonts and images are served from this site. The company that hosts it may keep standard server logs, like IP addresses, for security and to keep the site running.
